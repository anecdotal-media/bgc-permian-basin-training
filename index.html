Strategy 01

Why does anyone watch anything on social?

Three primary reasons:

01

Entertaining

Funny, surprising, satisfying. Makes them feel something pleasant in five seconds.

02

Educational

They learned something they didn't know. Useful, novel, "huh, I didn't know that."

03

Inspirational

It moved them. Made them want to be better, do better, give more, try harder.

Strategy 02 · Hooks

Earn the next three seconds.

If the first 1–3 seconds don't grab them, the rest doesn't matter. Four ways to land it:

Visual

Something interesting happening in the frame. The eye stops because something is moving in a way it didn't expect.

  • Open mid-action
  • Quick cuts in the first 2 seconds
  • An unexpected angle
Curiosity

Open a loop in their head and don't close it right away. The brain stays for the answer.

  • Tease a number — "3 things you didn't know about…"
  • Set up stakes — "She'd never picked up a ball until this week."
  • Pay it off at the end, not the start
Statement

Just say what's happening, plainly, in the first second. Sets the expectation. The right viewer stays.

  • "Today we're doing free-throw practice."
  • "This is what an after-school tutoring session looks like."
  • Cut to the action immediately
Question

Pose a question directly to the viewer, like you're talking one-on-one. Pulls them into the answer.

  • "Have you ever heard of…?"
  • "Did you know that…?"
  • Don't answer until you've shown them why they should care

Strategy 03 · The reflex

See it coming.

Some of these moments you can plan for. Some you can't. The job is to be ready for both.

01
Planned.Look ahead at your schedule and identify what's coming up that's exciting — that'll get a big reaction from kids, that has some suspense, that has natural drama to it. Walk in knowing you're going to capture it.
02
Unplanned.The lean-in, the laugh starting, the quiet moment of focus. When you feel one of these in the room, make it automatic — phone out, record. Don't think about it. The reflex is the whole job.

Strategy 04 · Spot the story

Four signals to stay alert for.

01
A firstFirst basket. First cooked meal. First day. First time speaking up. Firsts carry emotion automatically.
02
A surpriseThe shy kid leading. The athlete tutoring. Anything that breaks the assumption.
03
A contrastAn eight-year-old explaining code. A teen reading to kindergarteners. The opposite of what someone expects from "kids' club."
04
A real relationshipVisible trust between a kid and a staff member. You can't fake it. You can absolutely capture it.

Tactics 01

Think beyond the posed photo.

If everyone is staring at the camera and smiling, you've made a yearbook photo, not a story. Yearbook photos belong in yearbooks. Real moments — kids mid-laugh, mid-concentration, mid-handshake — those belong on social.

15 / 26
Tactics 02

Get in close. Tighter than feels comfortable.

Look at any YouTube thumbnail that gets clicked — almost every one is an expressive close-up of a face. The brain reads emotion fastest from faces. Eyes. Mouth. Reaction. The wider you shoot, the harder your viewer has to work to find the story. Get in. Crop in. Fill the frame.

Tactics 03

Shoot vertical.

TikTok, Reels, Stories, Shorts — every platform that matters lives in 9:16. Horizontal video posted to vertical platforms gets cropped, ignored, or stuffed into a tiny postage stamp. The only exception is YouTube proper — the long-form, full-screen kind. Everywhere else, turn the phone.

Tactics 03

Lighting in thirty seconds.

  • Light should be on the face, not behind it. If the brightest thing in the frame is the window, your subject becomes a silhouette.
  • Shade beats harsh sun. Outdoor at noon? Find a tree, a doorway, a covered porch. Even light, no squint.
  • Indoors, face the window. Free, flattering, professional-looking light. The window is your softbox.
  • If the room is dark, find a different room. Phones hate darkness — the footage gets grainy and muddy. Move.
18 / 26
Tactics 04

Audio is the biggest difference between amateur and pro.

  • Get close to your subject. Phone mic at arm's length picks up everything; phone mic at three feet picks up the room.
  • Find a quiet spot. If you can hear the AC vent or a basketball bouncing, your viewer will too. Step into a hallway.
  • Wait for the noise to pass. Whistle, plane, slammed door — pause five seconds, then go again. Free fix.
  • Capture the room. Ten seconds of background sound — kids laughing, ball bouncing — makes any edit feel alive.
19 / 26
Tactics 05 · Pair with b-roll

Let them tell. Then show what they're describing.

A talking-head video by itself is a podcast on screen. The same testimonial, intercut with footage of the actual thing they're talking about, becomes a story.

  • Parent says "my daughter is reading two grade levels above where she was last year" → cut to her reading.
  • Coach says "this team didn't know each other in September" → cut to a high-five, a huddle, a celebration.
  • Volunteer says "I come here twice a week" → cut to her walking in the door, hugging a kid.

Common pitfalls

A quick gallery of "don't."

Spot any of these in your own posts? Take it down and try again.

The press release

"We had a great day at the club!" — posed group + logo wall = no story.

The backlit selfie

Window brighter than the face. Subject becomes a silhouette.

Wide shot — most of the frame is empty
The super-wide

80% of the frame is empty room. The viewer has to hunt for the story.

0:00 — 0:03 — nothing happens
The slow start

Nothing interesting in the first two seconds. The thumb is already gone.

[ ... muffled ... ]
The far-away mic

Subject six feet from the phone. Nobody can hear what they said.

Underlit room — grainy footage
The dim room

Phones hate low light. Footage looks grainy and tired. Move toward a window.

After you capture · Edit

Make every second count.

Attention spans are short. Two moves in the edit:

01
Cut the wind-up.Don't open with "ok…" or "and we're rolling…" Trim until the video starts on the strongest second. If they speak, start when they start. Get in. Get out. Get scrolled-past less.
02
One CTA per post.Sign up. Share. Donate. Tag a friend. Pick one — don't ask for everything at once. A post with three asks gets zero of them.
